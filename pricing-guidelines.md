# Lake Salt — Pricing Guidelines

> **IMPORTANT:** These guidelines generate a *suggested quote range* for Kendell's review. The comms-agent must NEVER send a price to a client. Always present the suggested range to Kendell and let him set the final number.

---

## How Lake Salt Pricing Works

Lake Salt is a **dry-hire** mobile bartending service based in the **greater Salt Lake City area**. Alcohol cost is NOT included in the quote — the client buys their own alcohol. Lake Salt's quote covers bartending labor, setup, breakdown, equipment, and the premium service experience.

Pricing is based on three core inputs: **client type**, **bartender-hours required**, and **modifiers**.

---

## Step 1 — Determine Client Type (Pricing Tier)

| Tier | Client Type | Rate Per Bartender-Hour | Notes |
|------|-------------|------------------------|-------|
| **Corporate** | Companies, organizations, repeat business accounts | $350–500 | Higher budgets, repeat relationship value. Established accounts (e.g. Adobe) toward the top. |
| **Wedding** | Wedding receptions, rehearsal dinners | $175–250 | Premium event, high expectations, longer hours. Clients expect quality and will pay for it. |
| **Private** | Birthday parties, backyard events, dinner parties, private celebrations | $125–175 | Price-sensitive buyers. Keep competitive but don't undervalue the service. |
| **Nonprofit / Community** | Fundraisers, community events, charity galas | $100–140 | Discounted tier. Only apply when client explicitly mentions tight budget or nonprofit status. |

### Corporate Sub-Tiers
- **Established repeat client** (e.g. Adobe): Top of range ($400–500/BH). Pricing power from relationship.
- **New corporate client**: Mid-range ($350–425/BH). Competitive enough to win, room to increase on repeat bookings.

---

## Step 2 — Calculate Bartender Count

Use guest count and drink complexity to determine staffing:

| Drink Package | Bartender Ratio | Notes |
|--------------|-----------------|-------|
| **Beer & wine only** | 1 bartender per 75–100 guests | Simplest service, fastest pour times |
| **Beer, wine + cocktails** | 1 bartender per 50–75 guests | Cocktails slow down service |
| **Full service** (cocktails + champagne + custom) | 1 bartender per 40–50 guests | Custom drinks and champagne prep add significant labor |

### Additional Staffing
- **Champagne toast for 100+ guests**: +1 bartender for prep (pouring, staging glasses). Budget 30–60 min of prep time.
- **Frozen drink machine**: +1 bartender to operate and maintain the machine
- **Events over 200 guests**: Always minimum 3 bartenders regardless of drink package

### Minimum Staffing
- Every event requires at least 1 bartender
- Events with cocktails or 50+ guests: minimum 2 bartenders recommended

---

## Step 3 — Calculate Base Price

```
Base Price = Bartenders x Service Hours x Tier Rate
```

**Service Hours** = the client's requested bar service window (start to end). Setup and breakdown are included in the quote — do NOT add extra hours for them.

### Duration Adjustments
- **Events 4+ hours**: Apply ~10% discount on the hourly rate (long events are tiring but steady; slight discount helps close)
- **Events under 2 hours**: No discount — short events still require full travel, setup, and breakdown

---

## Step 4 — Apply Modifiers

| Modifier | Amount | When to Apply |
|----------|--------|---------------|
| **Bring the bar** | +$150–300 | Lake Salt provides the physical bar setup. Lower end for simple pop-up, higher for full bar with backdrop. |
| **Specialty equipment** | +$100–200 per item | Frozen margarita machine, espresso martini setup, draft beer system, etc. Includes extra ingredients/supplies. |
| **Custom cocktails** | +$75–150 | Signature cocktails requiring recipe development, specialty ingredients, or garnish prep |
| **Champagne service** | +$100–200 | Full champagne service (not just a toast). Includes glassware staging, multiple rounds. |
| **Rush booking** (< 7 days notice) | +15–20% of base | Short notice means scrambling for staff and supplies |
| **Travel fee** | Gas round-trip | For events outside the greater SLC metro area. Estimate $0.65/mile or actual gas cost. |

---

## Step 5 — Apply Minimums

### Minimum Event Fee: $450
No event should be quoted below $450, regardless of size or duration. Even a 1-hour, 10-person event requires travel, setup, service, and breakdown.

### Suggested Minimums by Tier
| Tier | Minimum Quote |
|------|--------------|
| Corporate | $900 |
| Wedding | $800 |
| Private | $450 |
| Nonprofit | $450 |

## Step 6 — Estimate Costs and Profit

Use these to sanity-check the quote before presenting to Kendell.

### Bartender Pay
Variable based on event complexity (not a fixed rate):
- Simple events (beer/wine, short duration): $30–35/hr
- Standard events (cocktails, 2–3 hours): $35–45/hr
- Complex events (full service, 4+ hours, custom cocktails): $45–50/hr

### Expense Estimate
Total expenses typically run **25–45% of the quoted price**, including:
- Bartender pay
- Supplies (cups, napkins, garnishes, ice, mixers)
- Equipment transport
- Gas/travel

### Profit Targets
- **Corporate events**: Target 55–70% profit margin
- **Weddings**: Target 45–60% profit margin
- **Private events**: Target 40–55% profit margin
- **Nonprofit**: Target 30–45% profit margin (accept lower margins)

If a quote calculation results in less than 30% estimated margin, flag it to Kendell — the event may not be worth taking at that price.

---

## Deposit & Payment Structure

- **Booking confirmation**: No deposit required immediately — gives clients breathing room
- **3 weeks after confirmation**: 10% deposit is due (non-refundable)
- **Remaining balance**: Due on or before event day (Kendell confirms exact terms per event)
- **Payment methods**: Venmo, Invoice

## Strategic Notes for Kendell

- **Wedding pricing**: $2,000–2,500 for full-service weddings is competitive. If deals aren't closing, it's likely a marketing/portfolio issue, not price. Consider raising to $200–250/BH — wedding clients associate higher price with higher quality.
- **Private event repeat business**: Individual clients rarely host multiple events per year. Focus on generating referrals from every private event rather than lowering prices.
- **The corporate relationship is gold**: ~55% average profit margin with repeat bookings at Adobe. Protect this relationship and use it as a case study for winning other corporate accounts.
- **Don't undervalue small events**: The $450 minimum exists for a reason. A 20-person dinner party still takes your evening — the business needs to earn for showing up.
- **Past underpricing**: Queen of SLC ($615 for 8 bartender-hours) and Anshu ($632 for 4 bartender-hours) were too thin. Future comparable private events should be $700–900 minimum.
