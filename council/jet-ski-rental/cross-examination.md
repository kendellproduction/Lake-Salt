ATTACKS ON THE "DON'T-BUILD" SIDE

A. The unit-economics advisor's utilization scenarios are asserted, not sourced. The "$300/day effective" rate, the 10/25/45-day scenarios, and "9%/72-84mo" financing are all placeholder-grade numbers (that advisor literally submitted key_facts: ["placeholder"]). Real 2026 dealer promo financing runs as low as 3.99-6.99% for good credit, and new Rec-Lite units start at $6,999 — so the $30k two-ski baseline is the most expensive plausible configuration, chosen to make the P&L fail. A steelman would price one ~$8k used unit at ~$180/mo and the pessimistic case shrinks from "-$7k/yr" to roughly -$2-3k/yr. The conclusion may still hold, but the model was tuned to the conclusion.

B. The opportunity-cost advisor double-dips the compounding argument. It counts the $850-950/mo pre-tax diversion compounded to "$350k-$490k by retirement" AND separately counts a possible lost employer match compounded to "$266k" — but the match loss is conditional on dropping below the match threshold, which nobody verified. If the user currently contributes well above the match, the true opportunity cost is the un-matched marginal dollars only. The number is directionally right and rhetorically inflated. Also, "$55k-$65k of contributions" assumes a full 60-month diversion; the friend's comp is 15 months. Using the worst-case duration while dismissing the comp's duration is inconsistent.

C. The liability advisor's strongest citation is its weakest link: "In re Luxury Jet Ski Rentals" reads like a fabricated or unverifiable case name — I could not confirm it exists. When your scariest exhibit is uncheckable, opponents will discount the whole lens. The underlying point (waivers fail under unlicensed-livery/negligent-entrustment theories) is legally sound anyway; it didn't need a possibly invented case. Similarly "FWC has actually charged individuals running informal FB rentals (survivalworld.com)" is sourced to a survival-blog aggregator, not FWC records.

D. All four con advisors assume the user is in Florida-like regulation. Livery statutes vary enormously; many inland states have no livery permit regime at all. Nobody asked WHERE the user's lake is — the single most outcome-determinative unknown (season length, livery law, insurance availability) — and every advisor filled that hole with the assumption most favorable to their position.

ATTACKS ON THE BULL CASE (build-modified, 0.55)

E. The bull's platform key_fact is factually wrong and load-bearing: "Boatsetter offers commercial rental coverage" for PWCs. Boatsetter's own support page explains it CANNOT insure jet skis/PWCs — its insurance partner excludes them (see fact-check #2). The bull case's entire de-risking mechanism ("platforms solve liability and demand") collapses for PWCs specifically. GetMyBoat will list a PWC but the owner must bring their own commercial policy — which is exactly the $1,500-$3,500+/ski cost the bull's cheerful "$500-$3,000+/yr obtainable" range soft-pedals at its low end.

F. The bull treats the coworker as "directly comparable local proof... same channel." It is not comparable: the con side correctly identifies the causal engine as a zero-CAC trust network the user admits they lack, and likely no insurance drag. The bull acknowledges this in its argument and then still cites the comp as validation in key_facts. That's motivated reasoning — you cannot keep the comp's revenue curve while adding the costs the comp skipped.

G. "Downside bounded by a liquid hard asset that recovers most of principal" is the bull's worst claim. A one-season rental PWC has high hours, renter abuse, and sells into an end-of-summer buyer's market; JD Power data shows Sea-Doos losing ~44% between years 2 and 5, and rental units trade below private-use comps. "Recovers most of principal" is unsupported. And the downside is NOT bounded by the asset at all — the unbounded term is uninsured liability, which the bull itself concedes in its biggest_risk.

H. The bull's "$4,000/month gross from 2 skis, 2 weekend days" arithmetic quietly assumes 100% weekend booking, zero weather cancellations, zero platform commission (GetMyBoat/Boatsetter take 10-35%), and the two-ski configuration its own modifications reject.

2. FACT-CHECKS (fresh WebSearch, Aug 2026)
═══════════════════════════════════════

1. "FB Marketplace bans rental/service listings" (Operations advisor) — CONFIRMED. Meta Commerce Policies prohibit services and rentals on Marketplace; such listings get pulled. The coworker's "FB Marketplace" channel was really FB Groups/word-of-mouth. This materially undercuts the idea's stated distribution plan AND the bull's "same channel" comp claim.

2. "Boatsetter's policy excludes PWCs entirely" (Operations advisor) vs. "Boatsetter offers commercial rental coverage" for PWCs (Bull) — CON CONFIRMED / BULL FALSE. Boatsetter's own support article ("Why can't I list a jet ski or PWC... under my own insurance") states its insurance partner (Buoy) does not cover jet skis/PWCs. The bull's key de-risking fact is wrong.

3. "Commercial livery insurance ~$1,500-$3,500+ per ski/yr" — CONFIRMED. 2025-26 sources (rentmy.co, rentrax, insuranks, cleverinsuranceguide) put small-operator PWC rental insurance at ~$1,800-$4,200/ski/yr, some quoting $2,000-$5,000. The con-side range is accurate, possibly conservative.

4. "Florida livery: permit required, first-degree misdemeanor without one, mandatory $500k/person / $1M/event insurance" — CONFIRMED. Fla. Stat. 327.54 (2024) and FWC livery regulations match exactly, including the renter-coverage-offer requirement. Caveat: applies only if the user is in Florida — state unknown.

5. "PWCs ~19% of reported boating incidents; blunt-force trauma, not drowning, leading cause of PWC death" — CONFIRMED (with nuance). 2024 USCG stats: PWCs = 19% of reported incidents. The blunt-trauma figure (~58% of PWC deaths) traces to trauma-epidemiology literature rather than the 2024 USCG report itself — directionally solid, citation slightly misdressed.

6. "New skis $6,999-$8,499 entry; used ~$4k-$9.5k" (Bull) — CONFIRMED. 2026 Sea-Doo from $6,999, Yamaha from $8,499 (jetdrift, watercraftzone).

7. "Yamahas lose only ~23% between years 2 and 5" (Bull) — CONFIRMED but cherry-picked: the same JD Power-derived data shows Sea-Doo losing ~44% years 2-5, and none of it covers high-hour RENTAL units, which depreciate faster.

8. "In re Luxury Jet Ski Rentals" (Liability advisor) — DUBIOUS. Could not verify this case exists; treat as unsourced until a real citation is produced. The legal doctrine it illustrates (waivers voided for statutory violation/gross negligence) is nonetheless standard law.
