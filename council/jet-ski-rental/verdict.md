# Council Ruling: Financed Jet Ski Rental Side Hustle

## 1. VERDICT: **dont-build**

Don't do this — not as proposed. The plan is two financed jet skis, marketed on Facebook Marketplace, with loan payments funded by cutting your 401k contributions. Every one of those three pillars failed scrutiny: FB Marketplace doesn't allow rental listings at all, doing this legally means commercial livery insurance that eats most of the margin on a 1-2 ski operation (and doing it *illegally* means betting your house and wages on the single most injury-prone vessel class in boating), and the funding source converts a marginal seasonal business into a raid on tax-advantaged compound growth. Your coworker's 15-month payoff was real, but its engine — a zero-cost, trust-based community network, almost certainly running without commercial insurance — is exactly the part you can't copy. There's a much better move sitting right in front of you: your existing Lake Salt Mobile Bar clientele, monetized via a referral partnership with an existing local operator, at zero capital and zero liability.

## 3. The Three Decisive Verified Facts

1. **Facebook Marketplace prohibits rental and service listings** (Meta Commerce Policies — cross-examiner confirmed). Your stated marketing channel doesn't exist. Your coworker's "FB Marketplace" success was really FB Groups + word-of-mouth inside a tight community you've said you don't have.
2. **There is no cheap insured path for peer-to-peer PWC rental.** Boatsetter's insurance partner explicitly excludes jet skis/PWCs (confirmed via Boatsetter's own support page); GetMyBoat will list them but requires you to bring your own commercial policy — which runs roughly **$1,500–$4,200 per ski per year** at small scale (confirmed across multiple 2025-26 sources). Personal PWC policies deny claims the moment money changes hands. So it's either real commercial-insurance drag that consumes the margin, or uninsured exposure.
3. **PWCs are the most dangerous vessel class to hand to strangers**: 19% of all reported boating incidents per 2024 USCG data despite a small fleet share, with blunt-force trauma (not drowning) the leading cause of death, and operator inexperience — the definition of a rental customer — a top contributing factor. In livery states like Florida, unpermitted rental is a criminal misdemeanor with mandatory $500k/$1M insurance requirements (Fla. Stat. 327.54, confirmed).

## 4. The Strongest Version of the Idea

If you genuinely want jet ski exposure, here's the best-case shape the council could construct — and note this is a *pilot*, not the proposal:

- **Buy:** ONE used, 2-3-year-old Sea-Doo or Yamaha with low hours, ~$6-9k, plus a used trailer. Past the steep depreciation cliff, small enough that a failed experiment loses low four figures, not $30k+.
- **Fund:** Cash or a small loan you can pay **without touching retirement contributions** — see section on funding below. If you can't, that's your answer.
- **Insure:** A real commercial livery/rental policy quoted *before* purchase (Prime, XINSURANCE, Global Marine — expect $1,500-$3,500/yr). Confirm your state's livery permit rules first. If the quote plus permit costs kill the math, walk away — do not run bare.
- **List:** GetMyBoat (with your own policy), local FB *Groups* (not Marketplace), and — your actual edge — cross-sell to Lake Salt Mobile Bar's event clientele: lake parties, bachelorette groups. LLC title, real signed waivers, safety briefing every rental.
- **Stop-loss:** If it isn't covering its own payment + insurance by end of one summer, sell it and be done.
- **Even better version:** skip ownership entirely. Partner with an existing local rental operator for referral commissions from Lake Salt events. That's the one idea every advisor independently converged on, and it survived cross-examination untouched: your clientele, their skis, their insurance, your zero dollars.

## 5. Concrete Next Steps (2 weeks)

1. **Days 1-2:** Look up your state's livery/rental law (search "[your state] personal watercraft livery permit"). If you're in a livery state, note the permit and insurance minimums.
2. **Days 2-4:** Get two real commercial PWC rental insurance quotes (Prime Insurance Company, XINSURANCE). This single number settles most of the debate.
3. **Days 3-5:** Check your 401k: what's your employer match threshold, and would the proposed cut drop you below it? Get the actual numbers, not vibes.
4. **Week 1:** Ask your coworker three blunt questions: what insurance did she carry, how many rental days per month did she actually book, and what did she charge. Her answers will confirm or refute the "uninsured community network" theory.
5. **Week 1-2:** Identify the 2-3 existing rental operators in your area. Contact them about a referral partnership with Lake Salt Mobile Bar (e.g., 10-15% commission on referred bookings, co-marketing at your events).
6. **Week 2:** Run the alternative math: what does booking one extra Lake Salt event per month net you? Compare to the jet ski base case. That's your real opportunity-cost benchmark.

## 6. What Would Change the Answer

- **The retirement funding question, explicitly:** this is the deal-breaker, and it stays a deal-breaker in every scenario. Cutting pre-tax contributions to free ~$700/mo after-tax costs you more like $850-950/mo of deferrals, and possibly employer match on top. Diverted over the loan term, that's plausibly $50k+ in contributions worth several hundred thousand at retirement — the true price of the skis. **If any version of this requires reducing retirement contributions, the answer is no.** If you can fund a small pilot from side-business cash flow or savings without touching the 401k, the modified pilot in section 4 becomes defensible.
- **A cheap insured path appears:** if a platform starts covering PWCs, or you get a commercial quote under ~$1,500/yr, re-run the math.
- **You're in a year-round Sun-Belt market** with a long season and no livery-permit regime — the base case moves meaningfully toward the bull's.
- **The coworker's answers reveal she was insured and permitted** and still cleared ~$2k/mo — that would mean the local demand is far stronger than modeled.
- **The referral partnership gets traction:** if Lake Salt events reliably generate rental referrals, *then* owning a ski to capture that margin yourself becomes a data-backed decision instead of a hope-backed one.
