# Council Recommendations — Business Ideas for You (2026-08-05)

## The Council's Short List

### 1. Lake Salt "Soda Social" — dirty soda / craft mocktail cart (Lake Salt sub-brand)
**What it is:** A second, smaller cart serving dirty sodas, Italian sodas, and elevated zero-proof cocktails — sold as an add-on at weddings Lake Salt already works, and standalone to the huge dry-event market you currently turn away: LDS receptions, church/school events, showers, daytime corporate.

**Why you:** This is the most Utah-specific expansion possible. Your planner/venue pipeline already fields dry-wedding inquiries you refer away today. Same sales motion, same booking system, same trailer logistics, same social engine. No liquor license means any family member can staff it after an afternoon of training — the one idea where "family wants to invest AND work together" actually fits.

**Cost:** $8-15K (used cart/trailer restyle, fountain/syrup setup, branding, permits).

**Year-1 numbers (corrected):** $600-800 average booking (real Utah comps: Thirst $600 min, Quenchit $795, Behind the Bar $3-4/pp — not the $1,000+ some scouts claimed). 40-60 events = **$25-45K gross at 60-80% margin**. First-year payback on capital.

**Hours:** Rides the existing weekend schedule; standalone Friday dry events staffed by trained family, not your Saturdays.

**Biggest risk:** Low barrier to entry, and Swig on Site is live in Utah at $749/175 drinks fully staffed — that's the price ceiling. Your defense is the planner/venue referral channel and boutique premium positioning, not the product. If referrals don't convert, this becomes a price grind.

### 2. Weekday corporate mixology + winter/Park City private events
**What it is:** Package your bartenders and gear into (a) hands-on corporate mixology classes ($65-75/pp is the confirmed SLC price anchor — Sip + Vibe), and (b) winter corporate/private chalet bar service in Park City sold through property managers and DMCs, with mocktail-forward packages where private-rental liquor compliance is murky.

**Why you:** Near-zero capital — it monetizes staff, knowledge, and equipment that sit idle on weekdays and all winter. Corporate and ski-season demand peaks exactly when weddings die (Nov-Mar). You are the product.

**Cost:** Under $3K (class kits, one-pager, insurance rider).

**Year-1 numbers (corrected):** Slow ramp — 1-2 bookings/month for the first two quarters while the B2B pipeline builds; $2-4K/month by the second winter. A 25-person class grosses ~$1,750 with two staff and ~$350 COGS.

**Hours:** Weekday evenings and winter — the exact inverse of your calendar.

**Biggest risk:** Corporate sales is a new muscle (longer cycles, gatekeepers, DABS permitting for some formats). Do NOT underwrite anything on Sundance — it moves to Boulder after January 2027.

### 3. Second Lake Salt bar unit (the "grow Lake Salt" play)
**What it is:** Clone the cart, serve 2-3 events per Saturday instead of 1, capturing the date-conflicted demand you already turn down.

**Why you:** It's the only idea family and prior council alike have already validated: grow the thing that works. Booked demand exists; $15-25K matches real build costs.

**Cost:** $15-25K — this consumes the whole family pool.

**Year-1 numbers:** 30 events × ~$1,800 = ~$54K new revenue, $12-16K profit at 25% margin — roughly one-year payback, *if* the turned-away demand is as real as it feels.

**Hours:** ~10 hrs/wk for 2-3 months training and documenting; then coordination, not bartending.

**Biggest risk:** The binding constraint isn't capital, it's a second *licensed, trusted bar lead*. Utah requires trained/certified alcohol service — family can barback, not run the bar. If you don't have that person identified, this idea stalls with capital deployed. (The "sell operator licenses" phase was killed: the brand doesn't travel, and no mobile-bar franchise exists anywhere — probably for a reason.)

### 4. Photo booth + audio guest book — attach-only add-on
**What it is:** A booth and audio guest book sold *only* on top of existing Lake Salt bookings (same truck, same trip, family attendant). Not a standalone rental business — that's the price-war zone.

**Why you:** Zero customer acquisition cost; sold in the same conversation as the bar. **Cost:** $6-8K. **Year-1 (corrected):** $600-800 booth + $300 audio book at 30-40% attach on ~35 events = **$10-14K/yr** at high margin. **Hours:** +1-2 hrs per event you're already at. **Risk:** attach rate — do not buy hardware until 3+ of your next 10 couples say yes at the quoted price.

## Killed Ideas Worth Knowing About
- **Coffee/espresso cart** — dominated by the soda cart on every axis; a big share of Utah's dry-event market doesn't drink coffee either, and bad espresso damages the flagship brand.
- **Christmas light installation** — real SLC average is ~$443/home (HomeAdvisor), not the $500-1,000 installer-blog number; saturated local market; roof-injury liability on family labor; zero use of your wedding-industry advantage.
- **Specialty decor/arch rentals** — numbers sourced from SEO content mills; ~$5-7K real net on $10-15K of trend-decaying inventory; competes with rental houses your planners depend on.
- **Tables-and-chairs micro-fleet** — commodity inventory vs. Diamond Rental-class incumbents; how $25K becomes a storage-unit bill. Keep only the booth.
- **Bounce houses** — vendor-marketing math (halve it), no unfair advantage, and it competes for the same family Saturday labor your real businesses need.
- **Operator licensing** — "Lake Salt" equity is worth ~nothing in Boise; the total absence of mobile-bar franchises anywhere is evidence, not opportunity.
- **Sundance-anchored Park City play** — Sundance leaves for Boulder after Jan 2027; folded the good parts into pick #2.

## How These Stack With the Venue Path
The endorsed operate-first venue track (management contracts / leases / revenue shares, bundling Lake Salt as in-house bar) is **strengthened, not competed with**, by every top pick:

- The **soda cart** makes any venue you operate bookable for dry events — in Utah that may double a venue's addressable calendar, and it's a bundling chip no competing venue operator has.
- **Corporate mixology/winter events** builds exactly the B2B sales muscle and DMC/property-manager relationships a venue-management pitch requires, and fills a managed venue's dead weekdays.
- The **second bar unit** is what lets Lake Salt be "in-house bar" at a managed venue *without* giving up outside Saturday bookings.
- The **photo booth and referral overlay** become in-house amenities that fatten a venue P&L you'd revenue-share on.

In short: the top picks are the vendor-stack a venue operator wants to walk in the door with. Pursue the venue conversations in parallel — they cost sales hours, not capital.

## Recommended Play — One First Move, One Use of the Money
**Put the family pool into the soda cart ($8-15K), keep $5-10K in reserve, and launch corporate mixology simultaneously with pocket change.**

Why the soda cart over the second bar unit: the bar unit needs a licensed lead you may not have and consumes the entire pool with no reserve; the soda cart costs half as much, is staffable by the actual family investors tomorrow, taps demand you're currently *referring to competitors*, and its every booking is incremental (dry events were never Lake Salt's to lose). It also doubles as the Silicon Slopes weekday product and the dry-event chip for future venue deals — three of the surviving ideas run through this one asset.

**Concrete 90-day sequence:** (1) This month: email your five closest planners and top venues — "we now do soda/mocktail bars for dry events, here's the package at $700-900" — and log response before buying anything. (2) If 3+ warm commitments materialize, buy and wrap the cart (~6-week build), train two family members. (3) In parallel, test photo-booth attach interest on the next 10 bar bookings (talk is free) and book two free/discounted corporate mixology classes with well-connected HR/DMC contacts to seed winter. (4) Revisit the second bar unit in Q1 2027 — funded by soda-cart cash flow, contingent on identifying a licensed lead bartender. One capital bet at a time; the family's money buys an asset the family can actually operate.

## Lake Salt 'Soda Social' — a dirty-soda / elevated mocktail cart as a second Lake Salt line

Build out a second, smaller cart (or restyle an off-the-shelf trailer/cart) serving dirty sodas, elevated mocktails, and zero-proof cocktails under the Lake Salt brand. Book it (a) as an add-on at weddings Lake Salt already serves, and (b) standalone at the huge Utah market Lake Salt currently cannot touch: dry LDS weddings, church and school events, corporate daytime events, and baby/bridal showers. No liquor license, no liquor liability, staffable by extended-family labor with an afternoon of training.

**Why you:** This is the single most Utah-specific expansion possible. Their pipeline of planners and venues already fields dry-wedding inquiries Lake Salt has to turn away today; the same sales motion, same booking system, same trailer-and-cart logistics, same social content engine. Family investors can actually work these events (no bartending skill or alcohol handling required), which fits the 'family wants to invest in something together' constraint better than any passive investment. A dry event booked on a Saturday Lake Salt wasn't going to win anyway is pure incremental revenue, not cannibalization.

**Startup cost:** $8K-$18K: used cart or small single-axle trailer restyled ($5-12K), commercial soda fountain/draft setup, syrups, smallware, branding/wrap ($2-4K), permits and initial inventory ($1-2K). Comfortably inside the $10-30K family pool with reserve left over.

**Revenue math:** Utah wedding soda bars are an established professional category with dedicated operators (You & Me Sodas markets itself as 'Utah's #1 Wedding Soda Bar'; Behind the Bar Soda Catering serves weddings and corporate events statewide), and packages in this category typically run roughly $700-$1,500+ per event for 100-150 guests. COGS on soda + syrup + cream is on the order of $0.50-$1.00/serving, so gross margin per event is 75-85% — better than an alcohol bar. 2 events/week average (Fri dry wedding + Sat add-on or shower) at ~$1,000 = ~$100K/yr gross on a cart the family staffs. Even a conservative 40 events/yr at $900 = $36K on <$18K invested — first-year payback.

**Hours:** Rides the existing weekend event schedule; add-on bookings piggyback on trailers already being hauled to the venue. Standalone dry events can be staffed entirely by trained family members, so it scales without consuming the owner's Saturdays.

**Biggest risk:** It's a low-barrier category — a cart and syrups are easy to copy, and several Utah incumbents already have SEO/Instagram head starts. Defense is Lake Salt's planner/venue referral channel and bundling, not the product itself; if referral partners don't convert, this becomes a price-competitive grind.

**Key facts:**
- The dirty-soda trend that started in Utah has gone national in 2025-26, driven by social media and reality TV (AOL/press coverage of the 'Mormon-born soda craze replacing coffee and cocktails across America').
- Dedicated Utah wedding soda-bar companies already exist and are thriving: You & Me Sodas ('Utah's #1 Wedding Soda Bar'), Behind the Bar Soda Catering (weddings, parties, corporate), Bottoms Up Mocktails (published plans/pricing) — proving willingness to pay, not just a listicle idea.
- Utah wedding press and TikTok treat soda bars as a standard reception category ('soda bar drunk' went viral enough to get national coverage), and couples increasingly book them as an intentional experience, not a consolation prize.
- No alcohol means no liquor licensing, no DABS compliance, lower insurance, and any family member can legally staff it.
