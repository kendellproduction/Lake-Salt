The 4.25-4.75% spreads apply to small loans. The "~$90K/yr debt service on $800K" figure is inflated by maybe 15%; still brutal, but the lens padded its numbers.

**4. "Seller note on full standby can cover half the equity injection, dropping cash to ~5%" (bull-case) — CONFIRMED but the bull buried the catch.** SOP 50 10 8 (June 2025) *tightened* this: the seller note now must be on **full standby (zero payments) for the entire 10-year loan term** and ≤50% of the injection. Pre-2025 it needed only 2 years' standby. Finding a retiring Utah venue owner willing to leave six figures interest-free and untouched for a decade is a far rarer animal than the bull implies. The capital-financing lens's claim that new rules made seller notes *more* usable is **FALSE-leaning** — the 2025 change made them harder to use, not easier.

**5. "Utah highest marriage rate, 24.5/1,000 in 2024" (bull-case) — CONFIRMED** (USAFacts; refined rate 51.7/1,000 unmarried women, 5th straight year #1). Note the market-monetization lens's "8.4 per 1,000" figure is a different (crude national-style) metric — its own fact list understates Utah demand threefold relative to the bull's sourced number.

**6. "22% of 2026 couples chose sub-30-guest weddings" (market-monetization) — DUBIOUS.** Could not verify. 2026 sources say 57% of couples *consider* a micro wedding but only ~6% actually hold sub-50-guest weddings. The oversupply/slow-bookings narrative itself is CONFIRMED (multiple 2025-26 vendor surveys report slower, budget-pressured bookings) — but the micro-wedding stat doing the quantitative work appears exaggerated or misread.

**7. "Utah average wedding ~$26K; venue rentals $2K-$10K, popular venues $3K-$7.5K" — CONFIRMED** (pix.wedding, wedding.report, La Fete). But one 2026 source puts the Utah venue line item at ~$8,500 average — the "Utah is too price-compressed" story is weaker than the dont-build lenses claim; venue spend is near national norms even when total spend is lower.

**8. "Utah venue listings start ~$720K, average ~$4.2M" (three lenses) — DUBIOUS as stated.** LoopNet/Realmo do list Utah venues, but I could not confirm the $720K floor or $4.2M average; these aggregate-listing averages are skewed by a few large event centers, and off-market/rural properties (the actual "unique property to renovate" premise) never appear in them. Three separate lenses citing the same unverifiable Realmo scrape is correlated error, not corroboration. Likewise the bull's "$100K BizQuest venue with seller financing": unverified, and at $100K asking on $150K gross it is almost certainly a leasehold business sale, **not real estate** — it doesn't support the ownership thesis.

## Part 2 — Weakest reasoning, both sides

**Against the dont-build bloc:**
- **Correlated, not independent.** Four lenses present the same single argument (capital gap vs. SBA down payment) dressed in four costumes, all resting on the same ProjectionHub/Realmo sources. Confidence should not be summed across them.
- **They price the wrong asset.** The premise is a *unique property renovated* — a house, farm, grange, church — not a listed turnkey "event center." Residential/agricultural property in Tooele, Cache, or Sanpete County at $300-450K with owner-occupant or seller financing is a categorically different capital problem than a $720K commercial listing. Nobody actually searched that market.
- **Debt-service double counting.** Market-monetization subtracts $79K debt service from a $46K "average net" that, per its own sources, is an average across venues *including* ones already carrying mortgages. You can't subtract the mortgage twice.
- **The opportunity-cost lens's "88% mobile bar margins" is gross-margin sleight of hand** — comparing a labor business's contribution margin against a real-estate business's net margin after depreciation and debt. Apples to oranges, and it ignores that the mobile bar builds zero equity.
- **Family-partnership lens is the strongest of the four** (securities exposure on many tiny LLC members is real and under-appreciated), but its remedy — "point the family at Fundrise" — ignores the actual family motive, which is investing *together in something theirs*, not buying REIT shares.

**Against the build-modified bloc:**
- **Both "build-modified" positions quietly delete the premise.** Capital-financing's plan is lease/revenue-share someone else's barn; bull-case's plan is "manage a venue for 3-5 years, maybe buy later." Neither is "buy a unique property and renovate it" — they are dont-build verdicts wearing a build badge, and should be scored as such. If the council tallies positions, this mislabeling manufactures false disagreement.
- **The bull's financing bridge is now largely closed** (see fact-check #4): the 5%-cash path via seller standby notes was gutted by SOP 50 10 8, and its cheap-venue evidence ($100K listing) is a leasehold, not property.
- **The bull hand-waves the fatal scheduling conflict it admits in its own risk line**: venue + mobile bar peak on identical Saturdays. A "modified build" that requires the user to be in two places at once is not modified enough.

## Part 3 — Net judgment

The council's *conclusion* (don't buy real estate now; leverage insider position first) survives scrutiny, but for cleaner reasons than most lenses gave: the true blocker is the **20% startup+special-purpose SBA injection (~$150K+ on even a modest project) against a $10-30K pool, with the seller-note workaround freshly gutted in 2025**. The dont-build side inflated rates, recycled one unverified listing average across four lenses, understated Utah demand, and never priced the actual asset class the user described. The bull side is honest about sequencing but dishonest about its label — and its capital shortcut no longer exists. Nobody on the council priced the one path that could genuinely change the answer: a sub-$500K rural residential/agricultural property with conventional or seller financing, which is where a real devil's advocate would send the next research pass.
