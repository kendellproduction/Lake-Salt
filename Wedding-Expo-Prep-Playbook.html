Booth & Design

Your booth: CORNER UNIT (10×10). Corners have one back wall (8' pipe & drape) and only one 3' side rail — the other side is open aisle. Two sides of exposure instead of one. Includes a skirted 6' table + 2 chairs + booth ID sign. Electrical and Wi-Fi are not included. Design for the two-aisle approach.
Corner-booth design mandate: You cannot use a standard one-sided booth layout. Both open sides need their own "front." Think of it as an L-shaped pop-up bar, not a single counter. Foot traffic hits you from two directions — if only one side is merchandised, you look closed from the other approach.

Corner Booth — Pros to exploit, pitfalls to avoid

✅ Corner advantages
  • ~2x traffic flow compared to mid-row booths
  • People pause at corners — natural photo-taking / gather spot
  • Less claustrophobic — guests don't feel cornered when they approach
  • You can do a true "pop-up bar" feel because you have real dimension
  • Better Instagram backdrops — content creation wins
⚠️ Corner pitfalls
  • Easy to have a "dead side" if design is lazy — always check from both aisles
  • Wind/traffic draft can knock over light props — weight everything down
  • Twice the sight-line = twice the mess visibility — hide prep/trash rigorously
  • Greeter has to pivot to face two directions — brief your staff explicitly
  • More surface area to fill — budget accordingly on props/florals

The Lake Salt Look

You're selling experience, not product. The booth has to feel like a pop-up bar, not a table with flyers.

  • Bring a full bar front — even a 4–5 ft bar façade with your logo reads as "this is what you get"
  • Back wall: tall branded banner (8' retractable) with your logo + one hero tagline + one call-to-action
  • Warm lighting (battery LEDs under the bar lip) — trade show fluorescents wash everything out
  • Live garnish display — citrus wheels, rosemary, edible flowers on a tray = photo bait
  • One signature cocktail in a branded glass, visible, pre-made, being handed to a human at all times
  • Neutral/earthy palette matches your brand — keep whites crisp, add salt texture props

What to AVOID

  • No music or speakers in the booth. ACS rule. Promotional silent videos OK.
  • No crowded booth — a wall of staff kills approachability. Max 2 people behind the bar, one greeter out front.
  • No price lists front and center. Price is a second-conversation topic.
  • No chairs facing outward — creates a barrier. Keep the bar open.
  • No business-card bowls as your only capture method (see Lead Capture section)
  • No generic "free consultation" CTAs — everyone says that

Electrical & Services — Do You Need Them?

Electrical is not included with your booth. If you plug anything in and haven't ordered it, you'll be shut off at setup. Decide now, order via the exhibitor kit — early-bird pricing is usually 30–40% cheaper than show-site rates.

The decision framework — do you need power?

Item you might runPower needed?Battery-powered alternative?Lake Salt call
LED under-bar / back-wall lightingOptional 5A/120V (~$100–150 early)Yes — USB/battery LED strips are $20, last 8+ hours🔋 Battery — cleaner, no cord management, looks the same
Mini fridge / ice keeperYes — 5A/120V neededNo — but insulated coolers + ice replenish works fine for a 4.5hr show❌ Skip the fridge. Coolers + pre-chilled product + one ice run.
Blender for frozen drinksYes — 10–15A/120VBattery blenders exist but are underpowered for a full day❌ No blenders. Sampling = pre-built cocktails/mocktails in shot glasses. No need.
Projector / TV screenYes — 5A/120ViPad on a stand works, charged beforehand🔋 iPad on a stand looping a reel = 80% of the impact, zero power cost
Phone / tablet charging for staffUseful but not requiredYes — 2× 20,000mAh portable batteries🔋 Battery packs
POS / Square for upsell booking depositsiPad runs on batteryYes🔋 Battery + hotspot
Kombucha/mocktail keg / draft systemYes — if anything is pressurized or chilledBottled service works for sampling❌ Bottled/pre-poured for expo sampling. Save the keg setup for real events.

My recommendation: skip electrical entirely

For a 4.5-hour sampling expo, you can run a fully battery-powered booth with zero sacrifice in visual impact. Saves ~$100–250 in services, eliminates a failure point (outlets not working, cords tripping guests, etc.), and simplifies setup/teardown.

But only skip if:

  • Everything you're plugging in has a charged battery or USB-power alternative tested at home
  • Your ice plan doesn't require a fridge (pre-chilled + insulated coolers + 1pm ice run)
  • You're not doing live blending, hot items, or kegged service (which you shouldn't be — it's sampling)

Order electrical IF:

  • You decide on a large backlit sign or illuminated display
  • You want a small fridge for non-alcoholic sample drinks you want cold all day
  • Your branded bar façade has built-in lighting that can't run on battery

Sampling Rules (Do Not Break These)

Sampling form + health permit must be on file. Exhibitors who don't have paperwork filed with ACS may be shut down on-site. You submitted in January — verify the ACS confirmation is in your inbox before the show.
  • Samples must match what you told ACS: what, how distributed, pre-packaged vs made onsite. If that's changed since January, email customerservice@acsshows.com now.
  • Sample sizes at bridal expos: typically 2 oz pours in compostable cups — enough to taste, not enough to be a drink.
  • Alcohol on the Salt Palace floor = extra Utah DABS permitting. Confirm with ACS + the venue whether your samples are alcoholic or mocktail. If unclear — ask in writing this week.
  • Hand sanitizer, gloves, hairnets (if required), covered ice source — health inspectors do walk booths.
  • Trash plan: one bin behind the bar + a runner to empty it. A full trash bin kills the vibe.

Lead Capture — The Full Plan

The math of this show. Bridal expos run ~800–1,500 attending couples. Even with a great booth, expect 80–150 direct booth interactions over 4.5 hours. Your goal isn't scans — it's qualified booked consults. Target: 60+ leads captured, 25+ marked "hot," 10+ consults booked within 7 days, 3–5 weddings closed.

Not your homepage. A single-purpose page optimized for one action: capture the lead. Build this on Firebase hosting alongside your existing site.

Tech stack: Firebase-hosted static page → form posts to Firestore expo_leads collection → Cloud Function triggers instant email (SendGrid or Resend) + Slack/SMS alert to you. Total build time: one afternoon.

B. QR code — design and placement

  • Generate one branded QR (use qrcode-monkey.com or similar — add your logo, make it brand-colored, keep 30%+ black ink for scanability)
  • Print 6 copies minimum: large on bar front, on the second banner, on both menu card stacks, taped to the clipboard, and on a small acrylic stand at the QR station
  • Test-scan all of them from 3 feet away before you leave the house show morning — nothing kills conversion like a dead QR
  • Use a UTM tag on the URL: ?utm_source=bridal_expo&utm_medium=qr&utm_campaign=bsl_s26 so analytics tracks it

C. Paper backup — the clipboard

  • Branded half-page sign-up sheet. Same 4 fields as the digital form.
  • Keep at greeter zone. When someone's interested but says "I'll scan later" — hand them the clipboard.
  • At end of day, photograph every page before packing up (insurance against losing the physical sheet).
  • Pen tied to clipboard. Always. Losing pens at expos is a universal law.

Step 3 — Qualifying on the floor (the 4-signal model)

Not every scan is a buyer. During conversations, listen for four signals. The more signals present, the hotter the lead.

SignalWhat to listen forWeight
📅 DateThey have a wedding date (specific month/year). Without a date, they're not buying yet.★★★★
🏛️ VenueVenue is booked. Bar-service shopping rarely happens before the venue is locked.★★★★
👥 Guest countThey know it within ±25. Signals budget conversations have happened.★★★
🤝 Decision makersBoth partners present, or the partner is consulted during the conversation. Solo-bride is still valid but slower to close.★★

Classification rule:

  • 🔥 Hot — 3–4 signals present, date within 12 months. Personal call within 48 hours.
  • 🟡 Warm — 2 signals present or date 12–18 months out. Email sequence, no call yet.
  • 🟢 Cold — 0–1 signals, "just looking." Add to newsletter, 3-touch nurture.

Mark every lead on the clipboard with 🔥 🟡 🟢 before they walk away. Takes 2 seconds, saves hours in follow-up sorting.

Step 4 — The scripted 45-second pitch

Your greeter and bartender need a tight, repeatable opening. Generic "Hey, want to learn about us?" fails at expos. Try this script:

  1. Open (5 sec): "Hey! Try a sip — it's our signature [drink name]. We're Lake Salt, we build custom mobile bars for weddings."
  2. Qualify (10 sec): "Are you working on a wedding? When's the date?"
  3. Story (20 sec): "We do the bar build, the menu, the bartenders, all the glassware — so the couple basically just tells us the vibe and we handle it end to end. [Reference something specific to their vibe if they mentioned it.]"
  4. Ask (10 sec): "I'd love to send you our menu + pricing — mind scanning this? Takes 30 seconds and you're entered in our drawing for a free Signature Cocktail Experience."

Never: pitch price unprompted, do a monologue longer than 60 seconds, or talk over the partner. If one partner's uninterested, engage them — couples close together.

Step 5 — The "in-the-moment" capture upgrade

For hot leads, don't just scan-and-bounce. Do this instead:

  1. After they scan, say: "While you're here — what venue did you book?" Write it on the clipboard next to their name.
  2. Pull out your phone, open Google Cal, say: "I'll save you a spot for a 20-minute consult — pick a time that works this week." Book it on the spot.
  3. Email confirmation fires before they leave the booth. Massive psychological lock-in.

On-the-spot consult booking converts at 3–5x the rate of "I'll email you later." This is the single highest-leverage tactic in your whole day.

Step 7 — The drawing (make it worth the scan)

The drawing prize has to feel real, not gimmicky.

  • Prize: "Signature Cocktail Experience" — we'll design a custom cocktail named after the couple, craft 50 of them for their wedding, served with branded stir sticks. $250 value. Costs you maybe $60 in actual ingredients.
  • Rules page: short, on the landing page. Drawing May 16 (one week after show). Email + social announcement. Must have a wedding date within 18 months to claim.
  • The reveal: announcement email AND Instagram post tagging the winner. Reinforces brand presence with every lead on the list.
  • Runner-up consolation: every non-winner gets 10% off their first booking if booked by end of May. Now the drawing is a full-funnel promo.

Step 8 — The Bridal Registration List (post-show windfall)

ACS will email the full attendee list (~1,200 couples) the Friday after the show (May 15). This is a major asset separate from your booth captures.

  • Import immediately into CRM, tag source BSL_S26_ACS_List
  • Match against your booth-captured emails — dedupe. Booth people get the "hot/warm" sequence, list-only people get a different "welcome from the expo" sequence.
  • Send one intro email to list-only: "We met at the expo last weekend — here's our menu + a 15% discount code if you book by June 1." Don't spam.
  • List is seeded (ACS tracks use). Do not share with non-exhibitors. Violating this gets you banned and fined.
  • Unsubscribes: respect instantly. Anyone who unsubscribes here is probably already not your buyer.
