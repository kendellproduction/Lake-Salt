## Executive Summary

Lake Salt is Utah's premier mobile bartending and events company. The brand sits at the intersection of **premium craft** and **genuine fun** — delivering elevated cocktail experiences that never feel stuffy or pretentious. Every touchpoint should feel like being served by the most stylish, skilled bartender at the best party you've ever been to.

The brand voice is confident and warm. Lake Salt knows what it's doing (TIPS-certified, fully insured, trusted by Adobe), but the energy is always inviting, never exclusive. Whether speaking to a bride planning her dream wedding or a corporate event planner booking a quarterly happy hour, Lake Salt communicates with polish, personality, and a deep respect for the craft of hospitality.

What makes Lake Salt distinctive is the blend: black-apron sophistication with real human warmth. The team shows up looking sharp, works with precision, and leaves guests smiling. The brand voice mirrors that experience.

### Voice Attributes Detail

#### Elevated
- **What it means**: Everything Lake Salt touches feels a notch above what you'd expect from mobile bartending. The setup, the cocktails, the uniforms, the menu cards — all curated.
- **How it shows up**: Use words like "craft," "curated," "signature," "bespoke." Describe experiences, not just services. Show attention to detail.
- **What to avoid**: Overly flowery language, pretentious wine-speak, anything that makes someone feel like they can't afford us or don't belong.
- **Evidence**: Website uses "Your Event, Perfectly Poured"; branded aprons with leather straps; printed cocktail menus at every setup.
- **Confidence**: High

#### Fun
- **What it means**: Lake Salt brings good energy. The bartenders are smiling, the drinks are creative, and the vibe is always positive. This is a celebration company.
- **How it shows up**: Conversational tone in client communications. Playful cocktail names. Exclamation points are welcome (sparingly). Show the team having a great time.
- **What to avoid**: Forced humor, excessive slang, anything that undermines credibility. Fun should feel effortless, not try-hard.
- **Evidence**: Event photos show bartenders laughing, engaging with guests; cocktail menus include creative names like "Dragon Fruit Mojito" and "Cucumber Sling."
- **Confidence**: High

#### Professional
- **What it means**: Lake Salt delivers what it promises, on time, every time. Certifications, insurance, and experience back up the brand.
- **How it shows up**: Mention credentials naturally (TIPS-certified, food handler certified, fully insured). Respond promptly. Provide clear, detailed quotes. Use proper grammar.
- **What to avoid**: Listing credentials like a resume. Sounding like a legal document. Over-qualifying every statement.
- **Evidence**: Website prominently features certifications; Adobe recurring corporate partnership; consistent branded uniforms across all events.
- **Confidence**: High

#### Approachable
- **What it means**: Clients feel comfortable reaching out, asking questions, and customizing their experience. The planning process is stress-free.
- **How it shows up**: Warm greetings, first-name basis, clear explanations of the dry-hire model, willingness to accommodate special requests. Ask about their vision.
- **What to avoid**: Jargon-heavy communication, making clients feel dumb for not knowing how bar catering works, impersonal form letters.
- **Evidence**: Website thoroughly explains the dry-hire model with transparency about costs; mocktail options show inclusivity.
- **Confidence**: High

#### Local & Proud
- **What it means**: Lake Salt is a Utah company that knows Utah venues, Utah liquor laws, and Utah culture. This is a strength, not a limitation.
- **How it shows up**: Reference Utah naturally — "across the Wasatch Front," "we know the best local craft beers." Feature local breweries (T.F. Brewing, Proper Brewing, Uinta) when relevant.
- **What to avoid**: Being parochial or defensive about being Utah-based. Never position as "small local business" — position as the local expert.
- **Evidence**: Logo features Utah state outline; event setups prominently feature local Utah craft beers; website lists specific Utah service areas.
- **Confidence**: High

## Messaging Framework

### Primary Value Proposition
Lake Salt brings craft cocktails, professional bartenders, and flawless execution to your event — so you can enjoy it instead of managing it.

Variations:
- "Your Event, Perfectly Poured" (tagline — use on website, social, printed materials)
- "Craft cocktails & mocktails, TIPS-certified bartenders, and flawless execution" (service description)
- "We bring the bar. You bring the celebration." (casual/social variation)

### Key Message Pillars

1. **Craft & Quality**
   - Core idea: Every drink is made with intention, skill, and premium ingredients.
   - When to use: Weddings, Instagram content, menu descriptions, proposals
   - Example phrasing: "Signature cocktails crafted around your story"
   - Effectiveness: High — this is the primary differentiator from "just hiring a bartender"

2. **Stress-Free Experience**
   - Core idea: We handle everything so you don't have to worry about the bar.
   - When to use: Wedding inquiries, corporate event planning, quotes
   - Example phrasing: "From setup to last call, we've got the bar covered."
   - Effectiveness: High — especially resonates with wedding couples and corporate planners

3. **Transparency & Trust**
   - Core idea: Honest pricing, clear communication, no surprises. The dry-hire model saves you money.
   - When to use: Pricing conversations, FAQs, initial inquiries, overcoming objections
   - Example phrasing: "You purchase the alcohol, we bring everything else — no markup, no hidden fees."
   - Effectiveness: High — this is a major conversion driver

4. **Inclusivity**
   - Core idea: Great drinks for everyone, including mocktails. LDS-friendly, all dietary needs welcomed.
   - When to use: Utah-specific marketing, wedding couples with mixed-drinking guest lists, corporate events
   - Example phrasing: "Craft mocktails that steal the show — because great drinks don't require alcohol."
   - Effectiveness: High — differentiator in the Utah market

### Competitive Positioning
- vs. Venue-provided bartending: More customizable, better cocktails, transparent pricing
- vs. Other mobile bartending: More polished brand, consistent uniforms, Adobe-level corporate trust
- vs. DIY bar: Professional execution, liability coverage, zero stress for the host
- vs. Full-service catering companies: Specialized bar expertise, lower cost for bar-only needs

## Tone-by-Context Matrix

Voice is constant. Tone flexes by context.

| Context | Formality | Energy | Technical Depth | Key Principle |
|---------|-----------|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| Wedding inquiry response | Medium-High | Warm | Medium | Make them feel special, show you care about their day |
| Corporate event proposal | High | Medium | High | Professional, efficient, prove reliability |
| Instagram caption | Low | High | Low | Personality-forward, visual storytelling |
| Instagram Stories | Very Low | Very High | None | Behind-the-scenes, real, in-the-moment |
| Quote/proposal email | Medium-High | Medium | High | Clear, detailed, builds confidence |
| Follow-up email | Medium | Warm | Low | Friendly nudge, add value |
| Website copy | Medium | Medium-High | Medium | Aspirational but accessible |
| Google Business reviews response | Medium | Warm | Low | Grateful, personal, brief |
| Client day-of communication | Low-Medium | Calm & Confident | High | Reassurance, logistics clarity |

### Context-Specific Guidelines

#### Wedding Inquiries
- **Overall tone**: Warm, excited, personal
- **Opening approach**: Congratulate them. Ask about their vision before quoting.
- **Do's**: Use their names, reference their venue if mentioned, ask about signature drink ideas, mention mocktail options
- **Don'ts**: Lead with pricing, use generic templates without personalization, rush to close
- **Example opening**: "Congratulations on the upcoming wedding! We'd love to help make your bar experience something your guests will talk about. Tell us a bit about your vision — do you have a vibe in mind for your cocktails?"

#### Corporate Events
- **Overall tone**: Professional, efficient, confident
- **Opening approach**: Acknowledge their event goals, demonstrate capability
- **Do's**: Reference past corporate work (Adobe partnership), emphasize reliability, provide clear timelines
- **Don'ts**: Be overly casual, oversell the "fun" angle at the expense of professionalism
- **Example opening**: "Thanks for reaching out about your upcoming event. We specialize in corporate bar service and currently partner with Adobe for their recurring events. Happy to put together a custom proposal — what's the event format and guest count?"

#### Instagram Captions
- **Overall tone**: Personality-forward, visual, inviting
- **Opening approach**: Hook with the visual or the moment, not the service pitch
- **Do's**: Show the team, the cocktails, the setups. Use 1-2 relevant hashtags. Be conversational.
- **Don'ts**: Over-hashtag, write long paragraphs, sound like an ad, use stock photo language
- **Example**: "That moment when the garnish hits just right. Dragon Fruit Mojito for 200? Yeah, we can do that."

#### Proposals & Quotes
- **Overall tone**: Clear, thorough, confident
- **Opening approach**: Recap what the client asked for, then deliver details
- **Do's**: Break down pricing clearly, explain the dry-hire model, include what's provided, set timeline expectations
- **Don'ts**: Use vague pricing, hide details in fine print, skip the personal touch
- **Example close**: "This quote includes everything you need for a seamless bar experience — bartenders, tools, mixers, garnishes, ice, setup, and breakdown. You just bring the bottles. Let us know if you have any questions!"

## Terminology Guide

### Must-Use Terms
| Term | Usage | Instead Of | Example |
|------|-------|------------|---------|
| Craft cocktails | Describing our drinks | "Mixed drinks" or "cocktails" alone | "We specialize in craft cocktails tailored to your event" |
| Signature cocktails | Custom drinks for events | "Custom drinks" | "Let's create signature cocktails that tell your story" |
| Dry-hire bar service | Our service model | "BYOB bartending" or "bring your own booze" | "Our dry-hire model means you purchase the alcohol, we bring everything else" |
| Bartender | Our team members | "Server," "staff," "worker" | "Your bartenders will arrive 90 minutes before guests" |
| Craft mocktails | Non-alcoholic options | "Virgin drinks," "non-alcoholic drinks" | "Our craft mocktails are just as impressive as our cocktails" |

### Preferred Terms
| Term | Usage | Example |
|------|-------|---------|
| Event | General term for any booking | "Tell us about your event" |
| Guest | People at the event | "How many guests are you expecting?" |
| Setup | Our bar station | "We'll handle complete setup and breakdown" |
| Experience | What we create | "We create a bar experience your guests will remember" |
| Curated | Describing menus or selections | "A curated selection of local craft beers" |

### Avoid These Terms
| Term | Reason | Alternative |
|------|--------|-------------|
| Cheap / affordable | Undermines premium positioning | "Transparent pricing" or "great value" |
| Party | Too casual for weddings/corporate | "Event" or "celebration" |
| Booze / alcohol (casual) | Too informal in client comms | "Spirits," "beverages," "alcohol" (formal) |
| Basic package | Sounds low-tier | "Essentials package" or describe what's included |
| Just a bartending company | Undersells what we do | "Mobile bar and cocktail catering" |

### Never-Use Terms
| Term | Reason |
|------|--------|
| Open bar / cash bar (without context) | Can confuse clients unfamiliar with bar models — always explain |
| Discount | Erodes premium brand; use "complimentary" or "included" if adding value |

## Instagram Style Guide

### Current State
Lake Salt's Instagram presence needs consistency. The brand has excellent photo content from events but lacks a cohesive posting strategy and visual grid.

### Recommended Content Pillars (rotate these)

1. **The Pour** (30%) — Close-up cocktail shots, pouring moments, garnish details. Hero content.
2. **The Setup** (20%) — Full bar setup shots before or during events. Shows the premium experience.
3. **The Team** (20%) — Bartenders in action, candid smiles, behind-the-scenes prep. Humanizes the brand.
4. **The Moment** (15%) — Guest reactions, toasts, cocktail handoffs, crowd shots. Social proof.
5. **The Menu** (15%) — Menu card close-ups, ingredient flatlays, cocktail of the week, recipe teases.

### Visual Consistency Rules
- **Filter/editing**: Warm tones, slightly lifted shadows, golden hour feel. Consistent across all posts.
- **Grid rhythm**: Alternate between close-up (cocktail/detail) and wide (setup/event) shots
- **Never post**: Blurry photos, cluttered setups, photos where the brand isn't visible, random non-event content
- **Branding in frame**: Aprons, menu cards, or the Lake Salt logo should appear in at least 70% of posts

### Caption Formula
- **Hook** (first line): A moment, a detail, or a question. Make them stop scrolling.
- **Story** (1-2 sentences): Context about the event, the drink, or the experience.
- **CTA** (last line): Soft ask — "Link in bio for your event" or "DM us your date"

### Caption Examples
- "Aperol Spritz station under the mountains. This is what Friday looks like when you let Lake Salt handle the bar."
- "200 guests. 3 signature cocktails. Zero stress for the bride. That's the whole point."
- "Behind the bar at Adobe. When corporate happy hour actually feels happy."
- "Your guests will remember two things: the food and the drinks. Let's make sure the drinks are unforgettable."

### Hashtag Strategy
Use 5-8 max. Mix of:
- Branded: #LakeSalt #LakeSaltBar #PerfectlyPoured
- Local: #UtahEvents #SaltLakeCity #UtahWeddings #WasatchFront
- Industry: #MobileBartending #CraftCocktails #WeddingBar #EventBartending
- Avoid: #follow4follow, excessive hashtags, irrelevant trending tags

### Stories Strategy
- **Day-of stories**: Behind-the-scenes prep, bar setup time-lapse, team arriving at venue
- **Post-event**: Highlight reel, cocktail close-ups, guest reactions
- **Highlights**: Organize into "Weddings," "Corporate," "Cocktails," "About Us," "Reviews"
- **Tone**: Casual, real, in-the-moment. Less polished than feed posts.

### Reels Ideas
- "Building a bar in 60 seconds" (setup time-lapse)
- Signature cocktail recipe walkthrough
- "What 200 guests looks like" (wide shot reveals)
- Bartender POV at a busy event
- Before/after of a bar setup
- "What we bring vs. what you bring" (explaining dry-hire)

## Language That Works

### Top Phrases
1. **"Your Event, Perfectly Poured"** — Context: tagline, headers, social bios. Why it works: concise, memorable, captures both service and craft.
2. **"We bring the bar. You bring the celebration."** — Context: website, social, casual copy. Why it works: simple split that immediately explains the dry-hire model.
3. **"Craft cocktails that tell your story"** — Context: wedding marketing. Why it works: emotional, personal, positions cocktails as part of the narrative.
4. **"From setup to last call"** — Context: service descriptions. Why it works: implies completeness and reliability without listing every detail.

### Questions That Engage
1. **"What's your dream cocktail menu?"** — Leads to: creative collaboration, client investment in the process
2. **"Tell us about your event — what's the vibe?"** — Leads to: personalized proposals, client feels heard
3. **"Have you thought about a signature mocktail for your non-drinking guests?"** — Leads to: upsell opportunity, demonstrates inclusivity

### Objection Handling Patterns
1. **Objection**: "We can just have our friend bartend"
   **Response pattern**: "Totally understand — a lot of people start there. The nice thing about having us is you get full liability coverage, all the tools and mixers, and your friend gets to actually enjoy the party. Most of our clients say that's the best part."
   **Why it works**: Validates their thinking, doesn't criticize, pivots to benefit

2. **Objection**: "That seems expensive for bartending"
   **Response pattern**: "Great question — let me break down what's included. With our dry-hire model, you're actually saving money compared to most full-service options because there's no markup on the alcohol. We bring bartenders, tools, mixers, garnishes, ice, and handle all setup and breakdown."
   **Why it works**: Reframes cost as value, highlights transparency, educates on model

---

## Language to Avoid

### Anti-Patterns
1. **"We're just a small local business"** — Problem: undermines premium positioning. Better: "We're a Utah-based team that's served everything from backyard birthdays to Adobe corporate events."
2. **"Our prices are competitive/cheap"** — Problem: invites price comparison, devalues craft. Better: "Our transparent pricing means you know exactly what you're getting — and you'll save on alcohol with our dry-hire model."
3. **"We can do whatever you want"** — Problem: sounds desperate, no expertise. Better: "We'll work with you to create a custom menu that fits your event perfectly."
4. **Excessive exclamation points!!!** — Problem: looks unprofessional. Better: One per message, max. Let the content carry the energy.
